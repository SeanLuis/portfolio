The blockchain landscape keeps evolving, with Ethereum and Hedera Hashgraph among the platforms shaping how decentralized applications are built.

## Ethereum: flexible and battle-tested

Ethereum introduced programmable smart contracts and remains the deepest ecosystem for DeFi, NFTs, and developer tooling. The move from Proof of Work to Proof of Stake improved energy efficiency. Typical L1 throughput is still modest (~15–30 TPS) without Layer 2 scaling, and finality can take longer than newer networks.

## Hedera: speed and efficiency

Hedera uses a hashgraph (DAG-based) consensus model aiming for high throughput (up to ~10,000 TPS design targets) with confirmations in a few seconds. Energy use per transaction is extremely low, and the network markets itself as carbon negative. Governance is council-based rather than fully permissionless in the Ethereum sense.

## Cost and sustainability

For many business workloads, predictable fees and fast finality matter as much as raw decentralization. Ethereum’s L2 ecosystem addresses cost/speed; Hedera packages those properties closer to the base layer.

## Takeaway

There is no single winner. Ethereum wins on ecosystem gravity and composability. Hedera is compelling when throughput, fee predictability, and enterprise-friendly consensus messaging are the product requirements. The “future” is likely multi-chain — choose the network that matches the trust, cost, and performance profile you need.
